## The Problem

Corporate business travel represents a substantial, tangible share of enterprise carbon emissions, yet organizations frequently treat it as unmeasured overhead or rely on generic annual averages. Without granular visibility into trip-level routes, cabin choices, and booking behaviors, sustainability teams struggle to pinpoint where emissions originate or evaluate the true impact of policy interventions.

Measuring carbon footprints at enterprise scale requires moving beyond static post-hoc spreadsheets to dynamic process analysis across tens of thousands of individual journeys.

The core objective: how do we leverage process mining and predictive modeling across real-world travel data to expose inefficiency and chart a viable, data-backed trajectory toward Net Zero?

## What It Does

This capstone analysis examines enterprise business travel operations through the lens of Celonis process mining and machine learning.

Across the dataset, the system:
1. **Process-Mines Travel Footprints**: Ingests and analyzes over 65,000 corporate trip records to uncover patterns in journey routing, booking lead times, hotel stays, and policy compliance.
2. **Predicts Trip-Level Emissions**: Trains a `RandomForestRegressor` model to predict carbon emissions (CO₂e) on a per-journey basis, achieving an R² of 0.9991, a Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) of 3.02%, and a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 25.00 kg CO₂e.
3. **Simulates Reduction Scenarios**: Models concrete reduction pathways through 2030—evaluating the cumulative impact of shifting short-haul flights to rail, enforcing cabin class caps, eliminating out-of-policy bookings, and establishing departmental carbon budgets.

## How It's Built

The pipeline combines process mining extraction with Python-based regression modeling using `scikit-learn` and `pandas`.

### Feature Engineering & Modeling
The predictive model was trained on the verified enterprise feature set extracted from the booking logs:
- **Location Attributes**: `DepartureLocationCountry`, `DepartureLocationCity`, `ArrivalLocationCountry`, `ArrivalLocationCity`
- **Transport & Service Details**: `ShippingType`, `ShippingTypeDescription`
- **Trip Metadata & Policy**: `Purpose`, `OutOfPolicy`, `BusinessUnit`
- **Cost & Duration Variables**: `HotelNights`, `NetCosts`

The dataset was partitioned into an 80/20 train-test split (`test_size=0.2`, `random_state=42`), allowing robust evaluation against unseen journey configurations.

### Feature Importance & Route Sensitivity
Tree-based feature importance analysis revealed that route geometry and travel class dominate predictive behavior:
- `ArrivalLocationCountry`: 36% importance
- `ShippingTypeDescription`: 23% importance
- `DepartureLocationCountry`: 17% importance

Together, origin-destination country pairs and transport modality account for 95%+ of the model's predictive power, confirming that high-level routing and flight class drive the vast majority of trip-level variance.

### Scale & Internal Consistency
When applied to 21,764 private corporate trips, the model predicted ≈58,690 tons CO₂e in total emissions and flagged 25.1% of journeys as high-carbon. This matches the public dataset's 25.0% baseline high-carbon rate almost exactly—a critical internal consistency check verifying model generalization across disparate booking batches.

## Decisions & Tradeoffs

1. **Proxying Distance via Service Categorization**:
   The source dataset lacked explicit point-to-point mileage or distance coordinates. Rather than discarding location data or attempting external API imputation, `ShippingTypeDescription` (e.g., specific flight tiers, train classes) was leveraged alongside departure and arrival cities as a reliable proxy for journey distance, transport modality, and cabin class tier.

2. **Selecting Random Forest for Multi-Type Tabular Data**:
   The feature matrix contained a mix of high-cardinality categorical attributes (city pairs, business units, booking purposes) and skewed numeric distributions (`NetCosts`, `HotelNights`). A Random Forest regressor naturally accommodated these non-linear interactions and mixed data types with minimal artificial encoding distortion, delivering high predictive accuracy without requiring extensive feature scaling.

3. **Public Validation vs. Unobserved Private Truth**:
   The model was validated against a held-out split of the public dataset rather than true private-trip emissions, which remain unobserved by design. While identical high-carbon proportions (25.1% predicted private vs. 25.0% public baseline) provide strong internal consistency checks, test-set performance serves as the best available proxy rather than a direct measurement of private-set ground truth.

## What's Next

The capstone synthesizes its empirical findings into a structured, three-phase recommendation roadmap to guide organizational decarbonisation:

![Net Zero 2030 Decarbonisation Trajectory](/images/projects/celonis-2030-trajectory.jpg)

- **0–3 Months (Visibility & Monitoring)**: Roll out department-level carbon tracking dashboards in Celonis to benchmark baseline emissions across teams.
- **3–12 Months (Policy Automation)**: Implement automated booking alerts that flag carbon-intensive itineraries and enforce out-of-policy exceptions before ticket issuance.
- **1–3 Years (Structural Decarbonisation)**: Mandate rail-first booking for high-speed corridors and link departmental travel budgets directly to Net Zero 2030 reduction targets.
