## The Problem

Most scroll-driven web animations swap between pre-rendered video clips or separate 3D models per section. This causes visual pop-in, heavy asset downloads, and rigid transitions that break when scrolling backward or at non-standard speeds.

Fracture was designed to solve this with a single persistent WebGL object that morphs continuously across material and structural states without swapping meshes or loading new assets.

## What It Does

Fracture features a central 3D geometric core that transforms across 5 material states as the user scrolls down the page:

1. **State 1 — Solid Crystalline Core**: High-refraction obsidian glass with sharp facets.
2. **State 2 — Voronoi Shatter**: The core fragments into dozens of floating polygonal shards driven by Voronoi math.
3. **State 3 — Liquid Mercury Morph**: Shards melt into a fluid, reflective metallic blob with organic noise deformation.
4. **State 4 — Wireframe Lattice**: The liquid crystallizes into a glowing holographic wireframe grid.
5. **State 5 — Re-assembled Monolith**: Particles condense back into a unified, polished monolith.

## How It's Built

Fracture is driven by a single custom GLSL Fragment and Vertex Shader applied to a single instanced buffer geometry.

- **GPU Fragment Shatter**: Voronoi cell decomposition calculated entirely inside the GPU fragment shader.
- **Simplex Noise Deformation**: Morphing between liquid and solid states powered by 3D Perlin/Simplex noise fields.
- **Seamless Scroll Sync**: Scroll delta maps directly to shader uniforms for frame-accurate forward and reverse scrubbing.

## Decisions & Tradeoffs

1. **Single Mesh & Shader System over Multiple Scene Swaps**: Eliminates loading hitches and memory spikes between sections.
2. **GPU Vertex Instancing over CPU Matrix Calculations**: Keeps main-thread execution free for 60fps UI layout updates.
3. **Ray-marched Glass Refraction**: Custom ray marching in fragment shader for authentic optical dispersion without heavy environment maps.
